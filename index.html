Building a D&D Character

Creating a character is one of the most exciting parts of Dungeons & Dragons. You get to design a hero and decide their story. It requires very few steps and uses more imagination then experience, so anyone can give it a try. There are also many resources to assist with character creation as well, such as DND Beyond

Choosing Your Character

Start by choosing a race and class. These choices determine your character's abilities, skills, and role in the adventure. Whether its a powerful mage casting spells from the back, a sneaky theif picking pockets, or a brave knight charging headfirst into battle. There are 13 classes to pick from, each with their own specialties, and too many races to count. All of the classes can be found here at WikiDot

  • Elf - A magical and graceful character.
  • Dwarf - A strong and tough character.
  • Human - A versatile character.
  • Wizard - A powerful magic user.
  • Fighter - A skilled warrior.

Choosing Your Abilities

Every character has six main ability scores that determine how well they perform different actions. Each ability has its own roll but not every character will be good at every ability.

Ability Description Example
Strength Physical power How easily your character can crush a tomato
Dexterity Agility and speed How easily your character can dodge a thrown tomato
Constitution Health and endurance How easily your character can eat a rotten tomato
Intelligence Knowledge and reasoning How easily your character knows that tomatos are fruits
Wisdom Awareness and insight How easily your character knows not to put tomatos in a fruit salad
Charisma Personality and charm How easily your character can sell someone a tomato based fruit salad

Creating a Backstory

Give your character a name, personality, and history. Think about their goals, friends, and reasons for becoming an adventurer! Think about how your character acts in certain situations. Give them flaws and things that have made them who they are. When you've finished your character you can draw them out or even design what they look like. Sites like HeroForge assist with this.
